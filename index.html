Hi! I’m Qi Sun (孙祺), and I also go by Albert. I am a senior undergraduate majoring in Computer Science at NYU Shanghai, where I work with Prof. Chen Zhao on large language models. I am also a research assistant at NYU Langone Health, working with Prof. Yiqiu (Artie) Shen on AI for radiology.

My research interests include:

  • LLM distillation and learning methods: transferring capabilities from large models to small ones, and turning weak supervision signals into stronger models.
  • Agentic search: teaching language models when and how to search, so that their answers are grounded in evidence.
  • Medical AI: retrieval and reasoning over radiology reports and clinical records.

I am also broadly interested in LLM safety and in how model architectures shape what models learn.

Currently, I am working on on-policy distillation for search agents and on retrieval over abdominal CT/MRI radiology reports for early detection of pancreatic cancer.

I am applying for Ph.D. programs starting Fall 2027. Outside of research, I enjoy fishing and cooking.

selected publications

  1. NeurIPS
    From "Weak" Signals to Strong Models: Preference Delta Aggregation with LoRA Merging
    Qi Sun, Siyue Zhang, Yulin Chen, Yuxiang Xue, Ru Peng, and Chen Zhao
    In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Training strong large language models (LLMs) requires high-quality supervision, which is often scarce. Recent work shows that paired preference data from weak-weaker model pairs (e.g., Qwen3 4B over 1.7B), despite the limited quality of individual responses, can provide an effective supervision signal through relative quality deltas, which we term a "weak" signal. This motivates a key research question: can multiple "weak" signals be constructively aggregated for improving strong models (e.g., Qwen3 8B)? To this end, we propose Preference Delta Aggregation (PDA), the first framework that derives a preference delta from each weak-weaker model pair, instantiates it as a LoRA adapter learned through preference optimization, and aggregates the resulting deltas via LoRA merging. To further mitigate directional interference during LoRA merging, we introduce Geometric Alignment Merging (GAM), a geometry-aware merging method that aligns adapter subspaces before aggregation, enabling more robust composition of diverse deltas. Evaluations on knowledge reasoning and agentic search benchmarks show that aggregating multiple "weak" signals pushes performance beyond any single signal, with further gains as additional signals are incorporated. Correspondingly, PDA with GAM improves the strong model by 6.8 and 7.3 points on average for knowledge reasoning and agentic search, respectively. It outperforms all single-delta and multi-delta baselines, exceeding the best single-delta baseline by 2.1 and 4.3 points. Further analysis attributes these gains to the effective composition of complementary capabilities encoded across distinct preference deltas.

  2. Preprint
    Search, Do not Guess: Teaching Small Language Models to Be Effective Search Agents
    Yizhou Liu*, Qi Sun*, Yulin Chen, Siyue Zhang, and Chen Zhao
    2026
    Abs arXiv Bib

    Agents equipped with search tools have emerged as effective solutions for knowledge-intensive tasks. While Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit strong reasoning capabilities, their high computational cost limits practical deployment for search agents. Consequently, recent work has focused on distilling agentic behaviors from LLMs into Small Language Models (SLMs). Through comprehensive evaluation on complex multi-hop reasoning tasks, we find that despite possessing less parametric knowledge, SLMs invoke search tools less frequently and are more prone to hallucinations. To address this issue, we propose a lightweight fine-tuning approach that explicitly trains SLMs to reliably retrieve and generate answers grounded in retrieved evidence. Compared to agent distillation from LLMs, our approach improves performance by 17.3 scores on Bamboogle and 15.3 scores on HotpotQA, achieving LLM-level results across benchmarks. Our further analysis reveals that adaptive search strategies in SLMs often degrade performance, highlighting the necessity of consistent search behavior for reliable reasoning.
